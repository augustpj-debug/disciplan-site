Last updated: October 2026

The short version

Disciplan builds you a short daily plan and tries to make it fit your real life. To do that it holds your answers, your plan, and what you ticked off.

  • We do not sell your data, and we never will.
  • There is no advertising in Disciplan, and no advertising network, analytics company or tracking tool receives anything. There is no third-party tracker in the app at all.
  • Nothing is shared with another person unless you turn it on and they agree, and then only the specific things you switched on.
  • You can see everything we hold, take a copy of it, or delete all of it, from inside the app: Profile → Privacy settings.

What we collect, and why

Everything below is held because a feature needs it. Nothing is collected "just in case".

When you make an account

  • Your email address and your sign-in details. Handled by Supabase Auth, our database provider. We never see your password.
  • The name you choose to display. A first name is enough, and it is the only part of your profile anybody else can ever see.

Your answers in onboarding and Profile

Your main goal and any second goals, what you want help with, how active you are, your kind of workday, how much time you have in a day, when your energy is best, what usually gets in your way, the tone you want to be spoken to in, any regular practice or game days, foods you avoid, your timezone, and the town or zip code you type in for the weather.

Why: these are what make the plan yours rather than generic.

The weather is by town or zip code, never by GPS. Disciplan does not ask for your location and does not read it. A daily high and whether it is raining does not need to know where you are standing.

Your plan and what you did with it

The plan built for each day, the kind of day it was, the tasks on it, which ones you finished or skipped and when, your morning energy answers, your streak, your end-of-day answer about whether the day was about right, and the rewards you have earned.

Why: the plan adapts to what actually happened. Without the history it would ask the same thing of you on your hardest day as on your easiest.

Things you add yourself

Events you type in — a shift, an appointment, travel, a day off — with the kind and the times. Meals you pick for the week. Meals you write down yourself, with your own notes about what goes in them and how to make them. Links to recipes you save. Items on your grocery list. Activities you type in that are not on our list.

Why: so a day can be planned around what is actually on it, and so your own cooking is in the app beside ours.

Your notes are yours. The app shows them back to you in lines and otherwise does not read them, score them, or suggest a different version of your cooking.

Your calendar, if you connect one

Only the kind of an event and when it runs. We store that something is a game, a practice, a work shift, travel, a holiday or an ordinary appointment, and the times it starts and ends.

We do not store the title, the location, the notes, or who else is invited. The title is read on your phone, for as long as it takes to decide which of ten kinds an entry is, and then dropped. Nothing else in Disciplan ever sees it.

You can switch individual calendars off, so a family or shared calendar never reaches the plan. The names of your calendars are read on your phone each time you open Profile and are never stored — the only thing kept is the id of one you switched off.

Health data, if you connect it

Workouts (the kind and how long), a daily step total, and how long you slept. Read only. We never write anything back. No heart rate, no routes, no medical records.

This has its own consent screen and its own policy, and it is entirely optional — the app works fully without it. Disconnecting deletes what was collected. Consumer Health Data Privacy Policy.

Your workouts, if you use the training plan

Your age, how much lifting you have done, what equipment you have, and anything you would rather avoid — jumping, overhead pressing, deep knee bend, kneeling, lying on the floor, gripping hard, running. Then each session built for you, which exercises you ticked off, and your one answer about whether it was too easy, about right or too hard.

What you would rather avoid is treated as health data, under the same policy and protections as the rest. It is checkboxes, never free text about an injury: Disciplan holds "would rather not press overhead", and never a body part, a diagnosis, or a sentence you typed about your shoulder. Holding less is the surest way not to misuse it.

Check-in photos, if you add any

A photo you take or choose after finishing something, and the day and task it belongs to.

Private when you take it. Sharing one is a separate switch on that one photo. Files are held in private storage, in a folder per person: there is no address that opens one without being signed in as you, or as somebody in your inner circle you shared it with.

We read nothing else from your camera or photo library — not where a photo was taken, not when, and not any other photo.

Your inner circle, if you turn it on

Who is in it, the invite codes you make, who you have blocked or reported, the cheers sent between you, and the meals you chose to share.

Off until you turn it on. See "What other people can see" below.

What we do not collect

  • No location. No GPS, no background location, no workout routes.
  • No advertising identifier, and no advertising.
  • No analytics SDK. There is no Google Analytics, Firebase, Amplitude, Mixpanel, Segment or similar in the app. Nothing you do in Disciplan is sent to a third-party analytics company.
  • No contacts. We never read your address book. The only way somebody joins your inner circle is by a code you give them.
  • No microphone, no photo library beyond the one photo you pick, no calendar titles, no message contents.
  • No free text between users. There are no messages in Disciplan. A cheer is one of five fixed phrases.

What other people can see

This is the part worth reading closely, because it is the only place one person's information reaches another.

Your inner circle is off until you turn it on. Adding somebody needs both people: one shares an invite code, the other has to accept. Either side can decline, remove or block at any time.

When it is on, somebody in your inner circle can see only:

What Behind
Your first name Having an inner circle at all
How many things you finished today — never which ones The "today's progress" switch
Your streak The "your streak" switch
A check-in photo The photos switch, and that one photo being shared
A meal you shared The meals switch, and that one meal being shared

They can never see: anything from Apple Health or Health Connect, any calendar event, the meals you pick for your own week, your goals, your onboarding answers, your training sessions, what you would rather avoid, your grocery list, or anything you wrote in a note you did not share.

That is not a promise about our code — it is a fact about our database. There are exactly two sets of rows another person is ever allowed to read, and neither has a column for any of the above. The rules are enforced by the database itself, not by the app asking nicely.

Blocking is invisible to the person blocked, ends the connection, and stops the same invite code working again. Reporting is private: the person reported is not told, and nothing about you is sent with the report.

Taking something back works immediately, with one honest exception. Unsharing a photo hides it, stops any new link being made, and refuses anybody who had not already opened it. Somebody who *had* already opened that photo could, for up to an hour, still be served the copy their own session fetched from our hosting provider's cache. They had already seen it; what is guaranteed is that nobody new gets it.

Who we share your data with

  • Supabase, the company hosting our database and file storage, stores it on our behalf under a contract that forbids any other use. They are a processor, not a recipient.
  • Nobody else. Not advertisers, not analytics companies, not data brokers, not insurers, not employers.
  • We do not sell your data. We have never done it and the business does not depend on it.
  • Reward partners are told only that a code was redeemed. They receive nothing about your activity, your health or your plan.
  • We will disclose data if the law requires it, and we will tell you unless we are forbidden from doing so.

How long we keep it

  • Your account and its data: while your account is open.
  • Calendar-derived events: the few days the plan needs, then removed.
  • Health figures: while the connection is on. Disconnecting deletes them immediately.
  • Check-in photos: until you delete them, or delete your account.
  • Everything: deleted when you delete your account, including the photo files, within 30 days including backups.

Your rights

From inside the app, Profile → Privacy settings:

  • See what we store — every kind of thing we hold, and how much of it.
  • Download a copy — all of it, as a file.
  • Delete your account and all data — a real deletion, not a flag.

You can also email us to ask what we hold, to correct it, to get a copy, or to have it deleted. We will answer within 45 days and tell you if we need longer. Depending on where you live you may have further rights; we apply the same standard to everybody rather than asking where you are.

Children

Disciplan is for adults, and the training plan is for people 18 and over. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 16 and will delete it if we learn that we have.

Security

Your data is held in a database where every row is tied to the account that owns it, and the database refuses a request for anybody else's rows rather than relying on the app to ask correctly. Photo files are in private storage reachable only by a short-lived link. Traffic is over HTTPS. We hold no payment details of any kind.

Not medical advice

Disciplan offers general wellness guidance. It is not medical advice, it does not diagnose anything, and it is not a substitute for a doctor or a physiotherapist. If something hurts, stop, and talk to somebody qualified.

Changes to this policy

If we change what we collect or what we use it for, we will say so in the app. If the change needs your consent, we will ask for it rather than relying on consent you gave for something else.
